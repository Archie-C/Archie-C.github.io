Answers · Question 1

What tasks would you solve with linear regression?

  • Predicting a continuous value (e.g., house price, temperature)
  • Understanding relationships between variables (e.g., effect of study time on exam score)
  • Identifying trends in data over time

Answers · Question 1

When would linear regression not be suitable?

  • When the relationship between variables is non-linear
  • Predicting categories
A few of you also put when the data is noisy or has outliers. But this is true for modelling in general, not just linear regression.

Answers · Question 2

1. Fitting the linear model

$$ \hat{y} = mx + c $$

$$ m = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2} = \frac{\text{Cov}(x, y)}{\text{Var}(x)} $$

$$ c = \bar{y} - m\bar{x} $$

Covariance is how much y changes with x. Variance is how much x changes with itself. The slope is how much y changes for a unit change in x. If you are not familiar with the derivation. I will release the full solutions later today.

Answers · Question 3

Question 3

$X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times (d+1)}$: $n$ samples, $d$ features and a bias column.

$y \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ is the output vector; $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ is the weight vector.

  1. Derive the OLS solution that minimises $\lVert y-X\theta \rVert_2^2$.
  2. Under what conditions does the inverse formula fail?
  3. Name one practical fix and explain why it works.

Answers · Question 3

1. Derive the OLS solution

The matrices:

$$ X=\begin{bmatrix} 1 & x_{11} & \cdots & x_{1d} \\ 1 & x_{21} & \cdots & x_{2d} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 1 & x_{n1} & \cdots & x_{nd} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \theta=\begin{bmatrix} \theta_0 \\ \theta_1 \\ \vdots \\ \theta_d \end{bmatrix}, \qquad y=\begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ \vdots \\ y_n \end{bmatrix}. $$

The objective function:

$$ J(\theta) = \lVert y-X\theta \rVert_2^2 $$

Answers · Question 3

1. Derive the OLS solution

Write the residual sum of squares as a quadratic in $\theta$.

$$ \begin{aligned} J(\theta) &= \lVert y-X\theta \rVert_2^2 \\ &= (y-X\theta)^\top(y-X\theta) \\ &= y^\top y\boxed{-y^\top X\theta-\theta^\top X^\top y} +\theta^\top X^\top X\theta \\ &= y^\top y\boxed{-2\theta^\top X^\top y} +\theta^\top X^\top X\theta. \end{aligned} $$

Answers · Question 3

1. Derive the OLS solution

The highlighted cross-terms contain $y^\top X\theta$, a $1\times1$ scalar:

$$ \begin{aligned} y^\top X\theta &= (y^\top X\theta)^\top \\ &= \theta^\top X^\top(y^\top)^\top \\ &= \theta^\top X^\top y. \end{aligned} $$

Taking a transpose reverses the order: $(ABC)^\top=C^\top B^\top A^\top$.

$$ \boxed{-y^\top X\theta-\theta^\top X^\top y}=\boxed{-2\theta^\top X^\top y}. $$

Answers · Question 3

1. Derive the OLS solution

Differentiate and set the gradient to zero:

$$ \begin{aligned} \nabla_\theta J &= -2X^\top y+2X^\top X\theta = 0, \\ X^\top X\hat{\theta} &= X^\top y. \end{aligned} $$

These are the normal equations. If $X^\top X$ is invertible:

$$ \boxed{\hat{\theta}=(X^\top X)^{-1}X^\top y} $$

Answers · Question 3

2. When does the inverse fail?

The formula needs full column rank: $\operatorname{rank}(X)=d+1$.

$$ \operatorname{rank}(X)<d+1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X^\top X \text{ is singular}. $$
  • One feature is an exact linear combination of others.
  • There are more parameters than samples: $n<d+1$.

This means there are multiple solutions if the inverse doesn't exist.

Answers · Question 3

3. Regularisation

Add a penalty (regularisation) on the weights, with $\lambda>0$:

$$ J_\lambda(\theta) =\lVert y-X\theta\rVert_2^2+\lambda\lVert\theta\rVert_2^2. $$

Setting the gradient to zero gives:

$$ \boxed{\hat{\theta}_\lambda =(X^\top X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^\top y} $$

Answers · Question 3

3. Regularisation

The reason it works is that the regularisation term penalises large weights which in turn gives a unique solution. Consider a 2D example:

$$ \hat{y}=\theta_0+\theta_1x_1+\theta_2x_2. $$

If the two features have identical values, $x_1=x_2=x$:

$$ \begin{aligned} \hat{y}&=\theta_0+\theta_1x+\theta_2x \\ &=\theta_0+(\theta_1+\theta_2)x. \end{aligned} $$

Which means that only the sum $\theta_1+\theta_2$ affects their contribution not the individual weights.

Answers · Question 3

3. Regularisation

With $\theta_0=0$, the prediction is $(\theta_1+\theta_2)x$.

$1$$3$$4x$$\lambda(1^2+3^2)=10\lambda$
$2$$2$$4x$$\lambda(2^2+2^2)=8\lambda$

Example · code

Calculate the loss in Python.

targets = [3.0, 5.0, 7.0]
predictions = [2.5, 5.5, 6.0]

squared_errors = [
    (target - prediction) ** 2
    for target, prediction in zip(targets, predictions)
]
mse = sum(squared_errors) / len(targets)
print(mse)  # 0.5

This is a displayed code sample; it does not run in the slide.

The squared errors are 0.25, 0.25, and 1.0. Their average is 0.5. Use a pre/code pair to preserve indentation and line breaks.

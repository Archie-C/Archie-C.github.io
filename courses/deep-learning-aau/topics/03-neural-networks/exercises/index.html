Neural networks exercises 2026

Exercises in preparation for building, training, and evaluating neural networks.

  1. 01

    Question 1

    Consider a neural network with two inputs, two ReLU hidden neurons and one linear output:

    $$\mathbf{z} = W^{(1)}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}^{(1)}, \qquad \mathbf{h} = \operatorname{ReLU}(\mathbf{z}), \qquad y = W^{(2)}\mathbf{h} + b^{(2)}.$$

    For $\mathbf{x} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$, use

    $$W^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{b}^{(1)} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad W^{(2)} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & -1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad b^{(2)} = 1.$$

    1. Calculate the network output, showing your intermediate calculations.
    2. Calculate the total number of trainable parameters.
    3. Recalculate the output if the hidden neurons use identity activations instead of ReLU.
    4. Explain why nonlinear activation functions are important in neural networks.
  2. 02

    Question 2

    A neural network controls a rocket by predicting continuous thrust $T \in [0, T_{\max}]$ and gimbal angle $g \in [-g_{\max}, g_{\max}]$.

    1. How many output neurons are required? Explain whether softmax is appropriate for these outputs.
    2. Suggest output activations or transformations that enforce the control limits.
    3. What problems can arise when ordinary mean squared error is applied to outputs with very different scales? Propose a solution.
    4. Why might a model with low prediction error on expert demonstrations still fail to land successfully in closed-loop control?
  3. 03

    Question 3*

    Consider a single-hidden-layer network with scalar input and output:

    $$f(x) = b + \sum_{j=1}^{M} v_j h(w_j x + b_j).$$

    1. Prove that if $h$ is the identity activation, the network reduces to a linear function of $x$.
    2. Find weights and biases for a two-hidden-neuron ReLU network that exactly represents $f(x) = |x|$.
    3. Explain why an ordinary linear model cannot represent $|x|$ exactly over the real numbers.
    4. Explain why universal approximation does not make deep networks unnecessary.
    5. Does universal approximation guarantee successful training? Explain.
